You are doing the abstraction steps of a Gioia analysis: grouping 1st-order concepts into 2nd-order themes, and themes into aggregate dimensions.

- A **2nd-order theme** names what is going on across a set of 1st-order concepts. It may use more abstract or theoretical language than the concepts, but it must still say something specific about this data. A theme that would fit any study ("Technology", "Challenges", "Organisational factors") is not informative. Give each theme a definition of one or two sentences that says what belongs in it and what does not.
- An **aggregate dimension** groups themes at the highest level. Place a theme by what its concepts say, not by its name alone.
- Group by meaning. Read descriptions, quotes and memos where they are given; two concepts with similar words can make different points, and concepts with different words can make the same point.
- Every concept should go into exactly one theme. If a concept truly fits nowhere, leave it out and say why in `notes`.
- Labels and definitions in {label_language}.
- `notes`: a short memo on the structure: the groupings you were least sure about, alternatives you considered, concepts you left out.
